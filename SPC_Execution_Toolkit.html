Foundation

Overview & brand voice in execution

SPC’s voice is calm, not urgent — warm, not sentimental — grounded, not abstract — purposeful, not performative. These are operational constraints, not stylistic preferences. They shape every email, partner pitch, social post, and on-park sign in this toolkit.

Apply in every asset

  • Lead with connection to place, not institutional achievement.
  • Show impact through real projects and named locations — General Sherman, Moro Rock, Cedar Grove, Hospital Rock.
  • Name partners and clarify roles. SPC supports the National Park Service; we do not act alone.
  • Let long-term commitment carry weight. Avoid urgency or scarcity framing.
  • Frame asks around shared responsibility, not guilt or obligation.

Avoid in every asset

  • Unsourced traffic or visitor numbers. If a stat isn’t cited, label it Hypothetical or Internal Baseline.
  • Olympic claims framed as a single number (e.g., a fixed “LA28 visitor surge” figure). Frame LA28 as a moment, not a stat.
  • Implying a formal NPS partnership status or dates that aren’t public.
  • Generic copy interchangeable with any park or nonprofit.
  • Tools we don’t have (Salesforce, HubSpot, dedicated CDP). Reference only the live stack in §Stack.
Stat hygiene rule. Every number in this toolkit is one of three things: Sourced with a footnote and live URL · Hypothetical illustrative target · Internal Baseline drawn from SPC’s own analytics. If you can’t tag it as one of those, take it out.

Foundation

Strategic priorities for the LA28 runway

The six bets the team is making. Everything else in the toolkit serves one of these.

Priority 01 · Email list as #1

Owned email is the only audience SPC fully controls. Every campaign, partner activation, and on-park sign should funnel toward an email signup before a donation ask. Constant Contact is already in place — what’s missing is consistent capture across the website, Field Institute pages, and on-park QR codes.

Priority 02 · “The Other California” positioning

LA, the coast, and Disneyland are one California. Sequoia is the other. This is the line we want to own during the LA28 moment — visitors flying in for the Games discovering that the largest trees on Earth2 are a half-day’s drive away.

Priority 03 · The near-loss / wildfire story leads

The 2020 SQF Complex and 2021 KNP Complex fires killed an estimated 13–19% of all giant sequoias 4 ft+ in diameter on the planet.3 That is the lead. It is true, it is sourced, and it is the most powerful framing for stewardship the park will have in our lifetime. Use it.

Priority 04 · B2B club logistics as conversion

Outdoor clubs at USC, UCLA, Caltech, Pomona, LMU, and Sierra Club chapters drive group trips. Make SPC the easiest organization on the planet to plan a group trip with: pre-built itineraries, group rate page, single Monday.com intake form, single named contact.

Priority 05 · LA28 landing page

One canonical page — sequoiaparksconservancy.org/la28 (or similar) — that holds the “Other California” story, a half-day-trip itinerary from LA, a Field Institute LA28-window booking module, and the email capture. Drives all paid + earned traffic in 2027–2028.

Priority 06 · Minimum Viable Activation

Every initiative below has an MVA: the smallest version we can ship in 14 days with the team and stack we have. If a section in this toolkit doesn’t name an MVA, treat it as not yet planned.

Pre-Campaign

Priority audiences for the LA28 runway

Audience priorities that came out of the team’s research. Treat these as priority audiences, not a fixed segmentation model — overlap is real and intentional.

USC + Olympic athlete network

Who: USC Olympic alumni, current athletes, confirmed LA28 participants and their networks.

Why they matter: USC has historically been one of the strongest Olympic pipelines in the U.S.4 LA28 is institutionally tied to USC venues including the LA Memorial Coliseum.5

Goal with this audience: credibility and storytelling reach — not mass conversion.

Southern California outdoor + active community

Who: hikers, climbers, trail runners, cyclists, fitness-oriented Angelenos. Members of Sierra Club LA chapters, university outdoor programs, REI Co-op classes.

Why they matter: already biased toward the product. Lower acquisition cost. Highest likelihood of repeat visitation and stewardship behavior.

Goal with this audience: participation, repeat trips, B2B group conversion through clubs.

Brands, creators, and Olympic-era travelers

Who: outdoor / wellness brands (e.g. Patagonia, REI, Allbirds, Hydro Flask), athlete-creators with digital audiences, and visitors flying in for LA28 looking for a non-LA experience.

Why they matter: bridge tourism + lifestyle. Position Sequoia inside the broader California experience around the Games.

Goal with this audience: earned media, co-branded content, paid co-marketing where mission-aligned.

How to talk about the audience model. Internally we may use these buckets for planning clarity. Externally — with partners, press, and on grants — talk about priority audiences for the LA28 runway, not a fixed segmentation framework. The buckets are a planning tool, not a public framework.

Campaign Execution · Short-form

TikTok, Reels, & Shorts for a small team

A cadence built for two interns + one staff lead, working on Canva and shooting on phone. Quality target is “authentic and on-brand,” not “polished.”

Cadence per intern

  • 2 TikToks / week
  • 2 Reels / week (cross-post the strongest TikToks)
  • 1 YouTube Short / week (cut down from a Reel)
  • Total: ~20 short-form units / month / intern

Format playbook

  • Place & person: 15-sec hook with a named location (e.g. “This is General Sherman”) + a real person on-camera.
  • One-thing-you-didn’t-know: a single sourced fact per video (no compounding claims).
  • Restoration in motion: trail or sequoia restoration footage with a stewardship beat.
  • Athlete vlog: phone-shot, unedited-feel, athlete narrates their visit.

Guardrails

  • Every video has one source or is labeled Hypothetical / Internal.
  • Every video ends in either an email signup or a stewardship action — never a donation ask first.
  • Voice rule applies: calm, warm, grounded, purposeful.
  • Captioned (accessibility + watch-time).

Post-visit

Visitor → donor funnel

Built on the donor-funnel framework: awareness → emotional connection → tangible impact → retention. Every link gets stronger when visitors understand exactly where their money goes and feel personally responsible for the outcome.

Landmark moments

Bring up giving when people are emotionally connected — at General Sherman (the largest living single-stem tree on Earth by volume2), Moro Rock, Tunnel Log, Tokopah Falls. QR codes on signs say “Scan to learn more about this place and support it” — never “Scan to donate.”

Impact-driven micro-asks

  • “$5 helps maintain 5 feet of trail.”
  • “$10 supports one day of conservation work.”
  • “This restoration area was funded by visitors like you.”
  • “Wildfire recovery here is ongoing. Here’s how to help.”

Specific donation tracks

  • Trail maintenance
  • Wildfire-recovery + sequoia replanting
  • Field Institute / education programs
  • Junior Ranger materials + accessibility initiatives

Mobile-friendly giving

  • Apple Pay + Google Pay enabled on donation form.
  • QR codes on park signs go to a single, mobile-first /give-from-the-park landing page.
  • Frictionless email-first capture so we can re-engage even if they don’t give that day.

Personalized follow-up

  • Constant Contact thank-you from a real human, not the org.
  • 3-week update: “Here’s the trail you helped fund” (before/after photo).
  • Wildfire-recovery progress note tied to the donation track they gave to.

Retention loops

  • Recurring monthly giving offered after the first one-time gift, not before.
  • Field Institute booking offer at month 6 to converted donors.
  • Annual report mailed (not emailed) to top tiers.

Intern program — day-to-day responsibilities

A two-intern program designed for the actual cadence of the marketing team. Interns work in Canva, Later.com, Constant Contact, Monday.com, and Google Workspace. They do not own paid spend or sensitive donor data.

Intern Role A · Content + Short-Form

Owns: 20 short-form units / month, monthly content brief, captions + alt text, weekly reactive day on TikTok / IG comments.

Daily: 30-min stand-up · 2 hrs shoot or edit · 1 hr captioning / scheduling in Later.com · async approvals via Monday.com.

Deliverables/month: 8 TikToks · 8 Reels · 4 Shorts · 1 platform performance recap.

Daily: 30-min stand-up · 2 hrs research / drafting · 1 hr Monday.com pipeline updates · 1 hr meeting support.

Deliverables/month: 10 first-draft outreach emails · 1 partner activation memo · 1 updated partner roster slice.
