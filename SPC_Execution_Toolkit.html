01 · Strategic priorities

Six priorities this toolkit is built around

These priorities shape every decision: what to say yes to, what to say no to, and where the small team spends its time.

Priority 1

Build the email list as the #1 owned channel

Email is the most effective channel for small nonprofits, and 48% of donors say it's their preferred way to hear from organizations. Email sign-up should be front and center: on the website, in social bios, on park signage, and in partner outreach. Not buried in the footer.

Source: Nonprofit Tech for Good, 2026 Email Marketing Statistics: "48% of donors cite email as their preferred method of hearing updates and appeals."

Priority 2

Position Sequoia as the "other California"

LA28 will flood media with the LA coastline and Hollywood. SPC's opportunity is the counter-image: ancient, vast, quiet. Every piece of LA28 content should make Sequoia feel like the missing half of a California trip, not a replacement for the city.

Priority 3

Lead with the wildfire recovery story

The KNP Complex fire (2021) and the Castle Fire (2020) killed a meaningful share of the world's mature giant sequoias. This is SPC's most distinctive story. Visitor and donor messaging should move away from generic "preserve nature" language toward something more honest: "We nearly lost these trees, and we're still in recovery."

Source: NPS, Giant Sequoias and Fire (Sequoia & Kings Canyon).

Priority 4

Open a group and organization outreach lane

USC Peaks, UCLA outdoor programs, Sierra Club chapters, REI Outdoor School groups, and corporate retreat planners are all within reach. A simple "Sequoia Group Trip Planner" PDF plus an inquiry form is enough to turn casual interest into bookings, without needing new staff.

Priority 5

Stand up an LA28 landing page early

One persistent URL (e.g. sequoiaparksconservancy.org/la28), not a one-off campaign, that captures search traffic, hosts the "LA to Sequoia" itinerary, collects email sign-ups, and becomes the link destination for every Olympic-era partner post through July 2028.

Priority 6

Define a quick-start partnership option

Every new partnership conversation should have a default lightweight option we can launch in under 30 days with no new tools: a co-branded itinerary post, a social media collaboration, a joint email send, or a Field Institute group day. Bigger efforts are opt-in, not the default ask.

02 · Audiences

Three working segments, not a fixed framework

These are working hypotheses to focus near-term effort. They'll be refined as real data comes in from email engagement, website analytics, and the Power BI dashboard currently in development.

Segment A · Reach & credibility

USC & the Olympic athlete network

USC Olympic alumni, current student-athletes, LA28-affiliated networks, and high-visibility individuals with strong personal brands.

Why this matters

  • USC is one of the strongest Olympic pipelines in the US and is deeply connected to LA28.
  • Highest credibility ceiling per hour of work, though not the best for mass participation.

How we activate

  • Athlete Ambassador Program (retired Olympians and LA28 participants) for social takeovers.
  • USC Athletics and alumni association engagement with curated Sequoia experiences.
  • Storytelling, not sponsorship. Coordination with Learfield (USC's media rights partner) is required to avoid conflicts.

Recommendation: focus on credibility and high-value relationships, not volume.

Segment B · Action & participation

SoCal outdoor & active-lifestyle community

Hikers, climbers, trail runners, university recreation groups, and digitally engaged outdoor users in LA and the broader Southern California area.

Why this matters

  • Already interested in outdoor recreation, making repeat visits the easiest outcome.
  • Most likely to model good stewardship behavior to others.

How we activate

  • Outdoor club partnerships: Sierra Club Angeles chapter, USC Peaks, UCLA outdoor programs.
  • Co-branded weekend trips with planning resources and stewardship guidelines.
  • Strava and AllTrails branded challenges (partnership agreement and budget required). Suggested future capability

Recommendation: focus on action, repeat visits, and stewardship adoption.

Segment C · Reach & positioning

Brands, creators & Olympic-era travelers

Outdoor and athletic creators, fitness and wellness brands, and visitors traveling to Los Angeles for LA28.

Why this matters

  • Bridges tourism, lifestyle, and outdoor recreation in one story.
  • Positions Sequoia inside a broader "California experience" travel narrative.

How we activate

  • Direct creator outreach (outdoor, wellness, athlete lifestyle), including existing supporters like Martin Henderson.
  • "LA to Sequoia" 4-day road-trip itinerary as the flagship landing page asset.
  • Distribution via tourism platforms, hotel concierge programs, and rental car partners.

Recommendation: focus on awareness and integration into broader travel narratives.

How we use these in practice

Working segments, not a rigid framework

These three segments are a starting point to focus the first 6–9 months of work. As email engagement, website analytics, and the Power BI dashboard deliver real data, expect one or two of these to split further (for example, "SoCal outdoor" could split into "weekend trail user" vs. "club or group leader") and at least one new segment to emerge from donor data.

Each segment has a different success measure: Segment A focuses on reach and credibility, Segment B on visits and repeat visits, Segment C on website traffic and itinerary downloads.

05 · Visitor-to-donor funnel

Strengthen every step, lead with email capture

Visitors are more likely to donate when they understand where the money goes and feel like their contribution made a real difference. This funnel is built on that principle.

1

Transparency first

Every tour and landing page opens with a clear, plain-language statement of exactly what SPC does. No jargon. "We restore, protect, and maintain Sequoia & Kings Canyon. Here's what that looks like on the ground today."

2

Day-to-day impact

Marketing leads with the workers: the crew clearing debris after a storm, the ecologist tagging a fisher, the irrigation tech fixing the drip line keeping a young sequoia alive. Visitors see the people before they see an ask.

3

Money made visible

Show the physical things donations buy. On the trail: "This irrigation pipe waters 40 sequoia seedlings, funded by donors like you." On the donate page, each track has a specific, tangible anchor (see tracks below).

4

Personalized follow-through

After every donation, the donor enters an automated impact journey: a thank-you within 24 hours, a 30-day update tied to their specific track, and a 90-day before-and-after photo. Constant Contact with simple segmentation tags makes this achievable with no new tools.

Tour transparency: open with honesty

Every guided tour, self-guided trail pamphlet, and digital tour page should open with a 60-second plain-language overview of what SPC actually does day-to-day. Not marketing copy. Operational reality. Visitors who understand the work before they're asked to support it are far more likely to give and stay connected.

What to say at the top of every tour

  • Who SPC is: the nonprofit that operates inside the park, not the park service, not the government. A small team that fills the gap.
  • What the team does daily: trail maintenance, seedling irrigation, wildfire replanting, junior ranger programs, and wildlife monitoring.
  • What the stakes are: the KNP Complex and Castle fires killed an estimated 10–20% of the world's mature giant sequoias. Recovery is active, funded, and measurable.
  • What visitors can do: one clear, easy ask at the end. Email sign-up or a specific donation track.

Impact tours: see exactly what your money funds

  • Replanting walk: tour the active replanting area. The guide points to seedlings, drip lines, and mulch beds installed with donor funding.
  • Trail crew shadow: small groups join a trail crew for 30 minutes to see the tools, hear the work scope, and experience the scale.
  • Wildlife recovery route: a ranger-led route through fisher and owl recovery habitat with live monitoring context.
  • Donor-dedicated signage stops: plaques or QR codes on infrastructure (pipes, mulch rings, bridges) showing what funded each one.

Marketing focus: the day-to-day work is the story

Most conservation marketing shows beautiful landscapes. SPC's advantage is showing the labor behind keeping those landscapes alive. Lead with workers and specific tasks, not scenery. Impact is most believable when it's concrete and specific.

Content angles that show impact

  • "This is what 7am looks like on the trail crew."
  • "We planted 200 sequoia seedlings this week. Here's what it takes."
  • "The drip irrigation that keeps a baby giant sequoia alive costs $8 a month."
  • "Meet [name], the ecologist tracking fisher recovery in Kings Canyon."
  • "Before and after: this slope after the KNP Complex fire, and today."

Formats

  • Short-form video: follow a worker through one task from start to finish (60–90 seconds).
  • Photo series: "A day in the life of a trail maintenance technician."
  • Progress updates: Instagram Stories or email showing replanting plot milestones.
  • On-site signage: QR codes at infrastructure linking to a 30-second video of that specific work being done.

Tone guide

  • Specific over vague: "42 seedlings irrigated this morning" beats "protecting the forest."
  • Worker voice: let the crew speak on camera. Unscripted is more credible.
  • Honest about difficulty: showing hard, unglamorous work builds trust.
  • No guilt: show the progress, not just the problem. Visitors should feel like they're joining a winning effort.

Donation tracks: split by specific purpose

Replace the generic "donate now" button. Each track below has a concrete, named purpose and a tangible cost so donors know exactly what their contribution does. Tracks should appear as distinct options on the donate page, shown as cards with photos, not a dropdown menu.

🌱 Replanting & seedling care

  • Funds: sequoia seedling propagation, planting labor, and first-year survival monitoring.
  • Cost anchor: "$25 plants and waters one sequoia seedling for a full growing season."
  • Tour tie-in: the active replanting area is a physical, visitable place. Donors can return and see their seedling.

💧 Irrigation infrastructure

  • Funds: drip lines, pipes, valves, and seasonal maintenance keeping young trees alive through dry summers.
  • Cost anchor: "$8 a month maintains drip irrigation for one seedling for a year."
  • Tour tie-in: walk donors past visible pipe networks with signage showing what was funded.

🍂 Mulch & soil restoration

  • Funds: mulch procurement, spreading labor, and soil health monitoring in post-fire areas.
  • Cost anchor: "$15 covers one seedling's mulch bed, insulating its roots through winter."
  • Tour tie-in: visible mulch rings around seedlings in replanting zones are a tangible proof point.

🥾 Trail maintenance

  • Funds: crew labor, tools, erosion repair, and accessibility improvements on high-use trails.
  • Cost anchor: "$5 maintains 5 feet of trail for a season."
  • Tour tie-in: trail crew shadow experiences connect this track directly to visible, in-person work.

🦉 Wildlife recovery

  • Funds: fisher monitoring, owl habitat support, and wildlife corridor maintenance.
  • Cost anchor: "$40 funds one week of fisher tracking and data logging."
  • Tour tie-in: wildlife recovery route tours bring donors into the habitat they're directly funding.

🎒 Junior Rangers & education

  • Funds: school group programs, junior ranger kits, and Field Institute youth curriculum.
  • Cost anchor: "$30 sponsors one child's full junior ranger day in the park."
  • Tour tie-in: group visits can include a live junior ranger activity as proof of this track in action.

Personalized impact follow-ups: automated but human

After a donation, the conversation should continue. A three-email automated sequence in Constant Contact, triggered by the donation track, turns a one-time gift into a relationship. No new tools required. Segmentation tags in Constant Contact determine which sequence a donor enters.

Email 1: Thank you (within 24 hours)

  • Confirm the specific track funded, not a generic "thanks for your donation."
  • One real photo of the work they're supporting. Not stock imagery.
  • One sentence on what happens next with their funds.
  • No second ask. Just acknowledgment.

Email 2: 30-day impact update

  • A brief update from the field tied to their specific track.
  • Example for the Replanting track: "This month we planted 180 new seedlings. Your contribution helped fund 7 of them."
  • A behind-the-scenes photo or short video from the work site.
  • A soft invite to visit the area in person (tour link or map).

Email 3: 90-day before-and-after

  • Side-by-side photo of a specific plot or trail before and after funded work.
  • A short quote from a worker about the progress.
  • A low-pressure re-engagement ask: "Want to stay on the journey? Here's how to give monthly."
  • Optional: AI-assisted personalization using first name and track in the subject line ("[Name], here's what your replanting gift looks like 90 days later").

Landmark moments

Place donation prompts at scenic spots where emotional connection peaks. QR signage example: "Scan to learn about this place and help keep it standing." Start with the highest-traffic landmarks first.

Mobile-friendly donating

Donation pages must support Apple Pay, Google Pay, or Venmo to minimize abandoned giving on phones. Friction kills conversions on mobile.

Email capture comes first

When it's a choice between a donation prompt and an email sign-up, capture the email. Email compounds over time; a one-time donation does not. Donor retention increases by 29% for offline donors when you have their email address. Source: Nonprofit Tech for Good, citing NextAfter.

06 · Content & short-form social

Small-team TikTok & Reels strategy

Built for a 1–2 person team. Volume comes from getting multiple posts out of one shoot day, not from being on every platform. Canva is the default design tool; Adobe is for deeper edits when needed.

Content pillars (rotate)

  • The giants up close: awe-inspiring moments at Sherman, Moro, Tokopah.
  • Near-loss and recovery: wildfire stories, replanting, science updates.
  • "Other California": LA28-era counter-image content for travelers.
  • Behind the work: trail crew, scientists, junior rangers.
  • Stewardship and how to visit well: Leave No Trace, accessibility.

Posting cadence (sustainable)

  • 3 short-form per week across TikTok and Instagram Reels (post once, cross-post via Later.com).
  • 1 carousel per week on Instagram or LinkedIn (data, behind-the-scenes, donor impact).
  • 1 longer video per month on YouTube (re-edit of the best short-form moment).
  • 1 Reddit post per month in r/NationalParks or r/CaliforniaHiking, value-first and not promotional.

Production model

  • One shoot day produces roughly 12 short-form posts. Always capture extra B-roll.
  • Phone-first capture is fine. Vertical 9:16 format by default.
  • Caption hook in the first 1.5 seconds. Voiceover should be scripted but sound conversational.
  • Use Bit.ly for every link to track clicks outside of Google Analytics.

10 short-form formats that travel

  1. "Things I wish I knew before visiting Sequoia."
  2. "This tree is 2,200+ years old. Here's what it has lived through."
  3. "Stuff you can only see in Sequoia, not Yosemite."
  4. "3 stops between LA and the General Sherman Tree."
  5. "Behind a single day of trail crew work."
  6. "How a giant sequoia survives a fire."
  7. "What we lost in the 2020 and 2021 fires, in numbers."
  8. "Ask the ranger" rapid-fire Q&A.
  9. Junior Ranger badge unboxing and what's funded by SPC.
  10. "Quiet park" sound-first edits for sleep or focus audiences.

Channel ownership

  • Instagram & TikTok: primary growth channels. Post identical Reels with platform-native captions.
  • LinkedIn: group outreach, donors, and partner discovery. Corporate-facing voice.
  • YouTube: long-tail search and landing page embeds. Monthly is realistic; weekly is not.
  • Facebook: older and family donor audience. Recycle Reels and event listings.
  • Reddit: trip-planning audience. Show up as a useful park, not a brand.
  • LinkTree: the central link hub from every social bio, pointing to the LA28 page, donate, group inquiry, and email sign-up.
07 · Intern program

Day-to-day responsibilities & deliverables

Designed for two part-time interns: ideally one from USC Annenberg and one from marketing or business. The goal is to expand what SPC can produce without adding management overhead.

Intern A: Content & social media

Daily

  • Monitor Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and LinkedIn comments. Flag anything sensitive to the staff lead.
  • Pull 1–2 short-form drafts from the existing footage library and queue them in Later.com.
  • Log yesterday's post performance in Monday.com (reach, saves, and link clicks).

Weekly

  • Publish 3 short-form posts and 1 carousel.
  • Refresh LinkTree links based on the week's priority asset.
  • Write a 1-page social recap (top post, lowest performer, one hypothesis for next week).

Monthly deliverables

  • One YouTube video (3–5 min) re-edited from the month's best short-form content.
  • One Reddit post in r/NationalParks or r/CaliforniaHiking, value-first.
  • End-of-month data pull from Google Analytics, Meta, and TikTok for the Power BI dashboard.

Intern B: Partnerships & donor outreach

Daily

  • Maintain the partner pipeline in Monday.com (status, next step, owner).
  • Research 2–3 new prospects against the partner checklist using only public contact routes.
  • Draft outreach emails using Constant Contact templates. Staff lead reviews before sending.

Weekly

  • Send 5–10 outreach messages across new and existing prospects.
  • Refresh the LA28 landing page with the week's new content or partner mention.
  • Update the donor follow-up list: who needs a thank-you, who needs an impact update.

Monthly deliverables

  • One Field Institute group inquiry recap (who came in, conversion rate, lessons learned).
  • One partner pipeline summary (stage by stage, biggest blocker, biggest unlock).
  • One A/B test on a Constant Contact send (subject line, send time, or call to action).
